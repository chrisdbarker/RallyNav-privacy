# RallyNav privacy policy

_Last updated: 28 October 2026_

RallyNav ("the app") is a turn-by-turn navigation app for Android phones and Android Auto
that reads directions like a rally co-driver. It is made by Chris Barker, an individual developer.

**In short:** the app has no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no crash reporting. The
developer does not collect, receive or store any of your data. What you put into the app stays on
your phone, except for what has to be sent to the map, routing and weather services listed below
to make navigation work.

## What the app uses and why

### Location
The app uses your precise location (GPS) to show where you are on the map, follow your route,
call the road ahead and work out when you have left the route. While a trip is running it keeps
using your location in the background through a navigation notification, so guidance carries on
with the screen off or in Android Auto. When no trip is running, location is used only while the app (or
its Android Auto screen) is open, to show your position on the map.

Your location is read through Google Play services' location provider, which is covered by
[Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

### Microphone
The microphone is used only if you choose to record your own co-driver voice (Settings →
Co-driver voice). The recordings are saved on your phone and are never uploaded by the app.

### Notification access (optional)
If you turn on "Music while driving", Android asks you to give the app notification access. The app
uses this only to see which music app is playing and to send it play, pause and skip. It does not
read, store or send the content of your notifications.

### Notifications
A notification shows your trip while navigating (time left, distance and progress). This is
required by Android for apps that use location in the background.

## Services the app talks to

To plan routes, search for places, draw maps and check the weather, the app sends some data
directly from your phone to these services. The developer never sees it.

| Service | What is sent | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [GraphHopper](https://www.graphhopper.com/privacy/) | Your start point, destination and any stops (as map coordinates), what you type into search, your location to rank nearby results, any point you drop a pin on (to look up its address), and your GraphHopper API key | Route planning, rerouting and place search |
| [WeatherAPI.com](https://www.weatherapi.com/privacy.aspx) (only if you add a weather key) | A few points along your route (the start, about every 25 km, and the destination) and your WeatherAPI key | Estimating road conditions along the route |
| [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/) | Requests for the map tiles around the area you are viewing | Drawing the map |

Like any website, these services can see your phone's IP address when the app contacts them. Each
handles data under its own privacy policy. All connections use HTTPS.

The API keys are your own: you sign up with GraphHopper (and optionally WeatherAPI) and enter the
keys in the app.

## What is stored on your phone

- Settings, including your API keys
- Saved places (Home, Work, favourites), your last 8 recent places and your parking spot
- Co-driver voice recordings, if you make any

Settings, keys, saved places, recent places and the parking spot are kept out of Android's cloud
backup and phone-to-phone transfer. Voice recordings can be included in your phone's own Google
backup if you have backup turned on. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage, deletes all of
it.

## Sharing

The app shares nothing by itself. If you tap "Share ETA" during a trip, Android's share sheet
opens with a message containing your destination's name, your arrival time and a Google Maps link
to the destination. You choose whether to send it and to whom.

## Android Auto

When you use the app in your car through Android Auto, it shows the same trip on the car's screen.
Android Auto is provided by Google and covered by Google's privacy policy. The app sends no extra
data because of it.

## Children

The app is a driving aid meant for adult drivers and is not directed at children.

## Changes to this policy

If the app starts handling data differently, this page will be updated and the date at the top
changed.
